# Product

## Product Vision

`پرسا | Pursa` helps Persian-speaking children practice philosophical thinking through culturally familiar stories, open questions, reasoning activities, counterexamples, perspective-taking, reflection, and family discussion.

## Problem Statement

Children often encounter rules, disagreements, fairness questions, truth questions, and friendship dilemmas before they have safe spaces to think them through. Pursa gives them structured opportunities to ask why, give reasons, listen to alternatives, and revise their views.

## Target Users

- Primary users: children in Iran, approximately ages 8-12.
- Supporting users: parents, caregivers, teachers, and facilitators.
- Contributors: Android developers, designers, educators, translators, and content reviewers.

## Educational Goals

Pursa should help children:

- Ask meaningful questions.
- Explain their reasons.
- Distinguish claims from reasons.
- Consider alternative viewpoints.
- Find examples and counterexamples.
- Revise opinions when better reasons appear.
- Disagree respectfully.
- Think critically, creatively, collaboratively, and carefully.

Pursa uses open-ended inquiry rather than moral instruction. The app should not evaluate children, infer personality, score opinions, or praise one pattern of changing or retaining views.

## Non-Goals

Pursa does not aim to:

- Teach memorization of philosophers, schools, dates, or definitions.
- Provide moral preaching with one predetermined correct answer.
- Replace teachers, families, or professional support.
- Offer social networking, chat, leaderboards, or competitive ranking.
- Collect personal information.
- Require internet access or registration.

## MVP Scope

The MVP should include:

- An offline Android app.
- Persian-first RTL interface.
- Authored story content stored locally.
- Open-ended questions and reasoning activities.
- Local progress storage.
- Local journal or reflection storage.
- Accessibility and privacy foundations.

The MVP must not include a backend, login, Firebase, cloud sync, advertisements, payments, push notifications, online AI, open-ended chat, voice uploads, or social features.

## Initial Worlds

1. Truth and lying
2. Justice and fairness
3. Friendship and loyalty

## Child Experience

A child should be able to open Pursa without registration, choose a story world, read or listen to a short illustrated story, answer open questions, compare viewpoints, think of examples and counterexamples, and reflect on whether their view changed.

## Family Discussion Experience

Family activities should invite conversation without requiring private disclosure. Prompts should be simple, respectful, and open-ended so adults can support discussion without turning it into a quiz.

## Success Indicators

Early success may be indicated by:

- Children can understand the story situations.
- Children give reasons rather than only choosing answers.
- Children can identify more than one possible viewpoint.
- Families can discuss prompts without needing special philosophical knowledge.
- The app remains usable offline.
- Privacy and accessibility principles are preserved.

These indicators are planning criteria, not claims of validated outcomes.

The current mission set has internal repository review against documented philosophical, editorial, safety, inclusion, artwork, and accessibility criteria. External educational review and child user pilots are still recommended before public release claims.

## Safety Principles

- Avoid sensitive personal disclosure.
- Avoid political or ideological propaganda.
- Avoid religious instruction.
- Avoid stereotypes about ethnicity, language, region, economy, gender, disability, religion, or family structure.
- Avoid shame, manipulation, or pressure.
- Keep content age-appropriate and emotionally safe.
