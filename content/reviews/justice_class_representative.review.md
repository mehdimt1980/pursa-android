## Central Philosophical Problem

The mission asks what makes representation fair: popularity, majority choice, competence, listening, or attention to quieter minority concerns.

## Competing Values Or Concepts

- majority and minority;
- popularity and competence;
- listening and leadership;
- procedure and responsibility.

## Defensible Positions

- Choose the candidate most classmates support because a representative should have broad trust.
- Choose the candidate who listens carefully because representation is more than popularity.

## Conceptual Distinctions

Voting versus fairness; popularity versus competence; speaking for others versus listening to others.

## P4C Structure

The mission includes classroom stimulus, initial judgment, reason prompt, charitable candidate perspectives, counterexample, reflection, and open ending.

## Perspective Quality

Perspectives avoid making the popular child shallow or the less popular child automatically ideal.

## Counterexample Quality

The changed case tests whether majority selection can overlook concrete minority concerns.
