## Central Philosophical Problem

The mission asks whether friendship begins through similarity, curiosity about difference, authenticity, politeness, belonging, or forced closeness.

## Competing Values Or Concepts

- similarity and difference;
- curiosity and comfort;
- authenticity and politeness;
- belonging and autonomy.

## Defensible Positions

- Start with shared interests because friendship often grows from common ground.
- Begin by learning differences honestly because pretending can weaken friendship.

## Conceptual Distinctions

Inclusion versus friendship; politeness versus pretending; curiosity versus pity; similarity versus compatibility.

## P4C Structure

The mission includes a concrete newcomer stimulus, judgment, reasons, two perspectives, pretending counterexample, reflection, and open ending.

## Perspective Quality

Both similarity and difference perspectives are charitable and child-plausible.

## Counterexample Quality

The pretending case tests whether appearing similar is enough for real friendship.

## Option Quality

Options avoid pitying the new child or treating quiet interests as inferior.

## Final Reflection Quality

Completion leaves room for similarity, curiosity, and respectful distance.
